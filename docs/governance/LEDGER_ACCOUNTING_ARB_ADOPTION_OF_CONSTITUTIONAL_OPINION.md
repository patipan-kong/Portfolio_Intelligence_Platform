# Architecture Review Board Resolution
## Adoption of the LA-WP2 Constitutional Opinion

**Convening body:** Architecture Review Board

**Matter:** Whether the Board adopts the LA-WP2 Constitutional Opinion as the governing constitutional interpretation for the current repository

**Resolution date:** 2026-08-03

**Artifact class:** Architecture Review Board interpretive adoption resolution

**Authority granted by this resolution:** `NONE` beyond the interpretive adoption stated herein

## 1. Disposition

`ADOPTED`

The Constitutional Opinion is adopted as the governing constitutional interpretation of the frozen Ledger & Accounting planning corpus for the current repository, subject to the constitutional scope stated in §3 and the limitations stated in §5.

Adoption is interpretive only. It changes no status, no authority, and no artifact.

## 2. Findings

### Finding 1 — Internal consistency

`CONSISTENT`

The Board examined the Opinion for internal contradiction and identified none. Three potential tensions were tested and resolved:

| Tested tension | Resolution within the Opinion's own text |
| --- | --- |
| The Opinion holds that no evidentiary standard is constitutionally fixed (§IV), yet upholds a block grounded in unmet proof (§I) | The block is grounded on failure of an express grant's object — Freeze competence reaching only "exact confirmed bytes" — not on failure of any prescribed standard. The two operate on different objects: silence on obligation, the block on competence (§II) |
| The Opinion permits variation in evidentiary model (§V), which could appear to permit an authority to relax the required conclusion | Foreclosed expressly at §V's first boundary: discretion runs to means, never to the required end. A model that cannot yield the identity conclusion leaves the Freeze grant's object unestablished |
| The Opinion holds that no contemporaneous identity-recording duty exists, yet upholds a Determination that recites non-recording as its cause | Resolved at §I(b) by construing the recitation as an evidentiary-state predicate, not a breach finding — a construction the Determination's own §3 compels |

The Opinion's structure is coherent throughout: every holding rests on either express text or the express absence of text, and its classifications (silence / ambiguity / delegation / expressly determined / foreclosed) are applied without cross-contamination.

### Finding 2 — Conflict with frozen planning artifacts

`NO CONFLICT`

The Board compared each holding against the two frozen artifacts (Plan 6e68ab3e…, Roadmap b812e31c…). The Opinion:

- adds no provision to either artifact;
- subtracts no provision from either artifact;
- enlarges no grant in Plan §4's role table;
- adds no trigger to Roadmap §5's terminal-state enumeration;
- adds no category to Plan §5's blocking-finding enumeration;
- adds no criterion to Roadmap §4's seven review checks; and
- treats Plan §4's rule against authority from silence as controlling throughout, rather than as displaceable.

Where the Opinion states a limit, the limit is traced to quoted text. Where the Opinion states an absence, the Board verified that the absence is an absence of text rather than a reading of text.

### Finding 3 — Conflict with the Governance Determination

`NO CONFLICT`

The Opinion affirms the Determination's outcome (`BLOCKED — GOVERNANCE`), its classification (planning-governance limitation), its scope limitation (LA-WP2 only), and its express denials (no implementation defect, no authority expansion, no constitutional contradiction). It disturbs no element of the Determination.

The Board records one construction carried by adoption: the Determination's phrase "under the current frozen planning corpus" is adopted in the sense fixed at Opinion §I — describing what is provable on the record, not asserting that the corpus commanded an act that was omitted. Adoption of the Opinion carries this construction. No alternative reading of that phrase is adopted.

### Finding 4 — Creation of new authority

`NONE CREATED`

The Opinion constitutes no actor, allocates no role, assigns no burden, imposes no duty, and confers no power on any party. It performs no review, confirmation, content-identity validation, freeze, closeout, ratification, allocation, or authorization. Its §V holding that evidentiary-model variation is permitted is a determination of non-prohibition derived from textual absence, not a grant — and it expressly declines to authorize any authority to impose a model on another.

This resolution likewise creates no authority. The Board notes for the record that under Plan §4 no actor may obtain authority from a document label, downstream need, or silence — a rule this resolution neither displaces nor may be cited to circumvent.

### Finding 5 — Adoption as interpretive precedent

`ADOPTED AS INTERPRETIVE PRECEDENT, WITH FIXED PRECEDENTIAL LIMITS`

The Opinion is adopted as the controlling interpretive reference for future constitutional questions arising under this same frozen planning corpus. Its precedential force is bounded as follows:

- It binds interpretation, not authority. A future competent authority remains competently constituted independently of this resolution; the Opinion is a reading of frozen text, never a source of power.
- It does not compel outcomes the frozen text does not compel. Where the Opinion classifies a matter as silence or as open, the precedent fixes that classification — it does not fix any particular resolution of the open matter.
- It is corpus-bound. It governs questions arising under Plan 6e68ab3e… and Roadmap b812e31c…. It carries no force over a different or successor corpus, whose interpretation would proceed from that corpus's own text.
- It is displaceable only by a competent later interpretive act, not by practice, assumption, or downstream need.

### Finding 6 — Recital variance recorded

The matter's recital summarizes the Opinion as concluding that "the frozen planning corpus is constitutionally complete." The Opinion's adopted holding is narrower and differently framed: that constitutional incompleteness is not an available classification, because the corpus supplies no completeness standard against which its own lifecycle stages could be measured, and that its silences are therefore not defects.

The Board adopts the Opinion's text, not the summary formulation. No affirmative finding of constitutional completeness is adopted, and none is entered by this resolution. This finding records a precision in what is adopted; it reinterprets nothing.

## 3. Constitutional scope

Adoption reaches only the constitutional interpretation of the frozen planning corpus — Plan 6e68ab3e3f152d105e72bef4f84cd7c4afae9e1a and Roadmap b812e31cb0473c16c324419e1efb6103af1e274a.

Adoption does not reach:

- the content, bytes, or identities of any frozen artifact;
- the status, disposition, or record of any work package;
- any allocation, authorization, review, confirmation, content-identity validation, freeze, ratification, or closeout;
- any implementation artifact, source code, runtime behavior, schema, persistence, API, or production method;
- M45, G-3, or any external downstream consumer's allocation or authorization; or
- the adequacy, sufficiency, or correctness of any evidentiary model.

Stated explicitly and without qualification:

- No governance amendment occurs.
- No implementation authority changes.

## 5. Limitations

This resolution is an interpretive adoption. It is not implementation review, constitutional interpretation, successor planning, or planning amendment.

The Board did not reinterpret the existing records recited in the matter. It examined the Constitutional Opinion for internal consistency and for conflict, and adopted it as written.

The Board verified no factual assertion in any underlying record — including whether identity was in fact unrecorded or is in fact unprovable. Adoption is of an interpretation, not of a factual finding.

No evidentiary model is recommended, preferred, ranked, or excluded. No governance arrangement is evaluated as desirable or undesirable.

No recommendation of successor planning is made, and none should be inferred from any statement herein. That the Opinion notes successor lifecycle as the corpus's own mechanism for material change is a description of frozen text, not a recommendation to invoke it.

This resolution determines nothing about any work package other than as stated, nothing about any past or future exercise of Freeze authority, and nothing about any question arising under a corpus other than the two frozen artifacts identified in §3.

This resolution performs no review, confirmation, content-identity validation, freeze, closeout, ratification, allocation, or authorization. It amends nothing, constitutes no actor, and grants no authority.

**Resolution:** `ADOPTED`.
